# Risk assessment

## Risks in the analysis itself

| risk | severity | mitigation in place |
|---|---|---|
| Two data points measure a rate, not a shape | **high** | confidence is capped at LOW by the type; projections are given as a linear-to-compound band rather than a line |
| Stars measure attention, not adoption or revenue | **high** | registry evidence collected where reachable; the limitation is stated wherever a star count appears |
| Three of five domains in the source scrape are empty | **high** | reported as UNUSABLE; conclusions scoped to AI Agent and RAG |
| Mined features are claims, not implementations | medium | capped at MEDIUM confidence in code |
| Architecture is inferred from manifests | medium | every inference carries the manifest; absent manifest yields UNKNOWN |
| Evidence ages from the moment it is committed | medium | the file is dated in its own filename |
| Revenue figures are judgement | medium | inputs are printed with every estimate |

## Risks in the market

**Licence exposure.** 4 of the Tier 1 projects cannot be built on: a
non-commercial research licence, a delayed-open licence, and a repository with no
licence file at all. Absorbing any of them without checking would be an expensive
mistake, which is why absorbability is a recorded field rather than an assumption.

**Stall risk.** 2 projects in the set grew under 5% across the window.
A stalled project is usually still maintained and still usable; what it is not is a
bet on the future, and an acquisition or partnership decision turns on that.

**Category risk.** The fastest growth is in small projects, which means position in
this market is not yet fixed — favourable for entering, and equally favourable for
whoever enters after us.

**Concentration risk.** Almost every project in the signal set is built around a
single agent runtime's ecosystem. That is where the users are today and it is a single
point of failure for the whole category, including for anything we ship into it.
